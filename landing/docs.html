01 — Getting Started

Getting Started

Connect your league and get started in under two minutes.

What it's for

Getting Started walks you through connecting your ESPN or Yahoo league to Roto Intel. Once connected, your roster, matchups, league settings and opponent data are all pulled in automatically.

How to read it
  • Connect your league from the Account settings (person icon, top right). ESPN uses a cookie-based method — see the ESPN connection guide for full steps. Yahoo uses a one-click login.
  • Once connected, ownership badges (Mine, Taken, Free Agent) appear across the entire platform against every player.
  • Your league's scoring settings are automatically detected and applied to all rankings and projections.
02 — ESPN

Connecting Your ESPN League

ESPN doesn't offer a simple one-click login for third-party apps, so the connection takes a few extra steps. Don't let that put you off — it takes about two minutes and you only ever do it once.

What you'll need

Three things from ESPN: two authentication tokens called espn_s2 and SWID, plus your League ID. These live in your browser when you're logged into ESPN — the steps below show you exactly where to find them.

Step 1 — Log in to ESPN
  1. 1

    Open Google Chrome and go to espn.com/fantasy/basketball.

  2. 2

    Sign in with your ESPN account — the same one you use to manage your fantasy team.

  3. 3

    Click into your fantasy league so you can see your team. Look at the URL in the address bar — you'll see a number after leagueId=. That's your League ID. Write it down or copy it.

Step 2 — Open browser DevTools

This is the part that sounds scary but isn't. DevTools is just a panel built into every browser that lets you inspect what's happening behind the scenes.

  1. 4

    While still on the ESPN page, press F12 on your keyboard (or right-click anywhere on the page and choose Inspect). A panel will open — usually on the right side or bottom of your screen.

  2. 5

    At the top of that panel, click the tab labelled Application. If you don't see it, look for a >> arrow — it may be hidden behind other tabs.

  3. 6

    In the left-hand sidebar of the Application panel, find Cookies and click the arrow to expand it. Then click on https://www.espn.com.

Step 3 — Copy your tokens
  1. 7

    You'll see a table of cookies. In the Name column, find espn_s2. Click on it and copy the long string of text in the Value column — this is your first token.

  2. 8

    Now find SWID in the same list. Copy its value — it will look something like {"{XXXXXXXX-XXXX-XXXX-XXXX-XXXXXXXXXXXX}"} with curly braces. Copy the whole thing including the braces.

Step 4 — Connect in Roto Intel
  1. 9

    In Roto Intel, click the person icon in the top-right corner to open Account settings.

  2. 10

    Under Fantasy Connections, click Connect ESPN.

  3. 11

    Paste your espn_s2 value, your SWID value, and your League ID into the three fields, then click Connect.

  4. 12

    Done. Roto Intel will sync your roster and league settings. Ownership badges will appear across the platform and your Fantasy Dashboard will populate with your team data.

Important notes
Notes
  • Your ESPN password is never entered into or stored by Roto Intel — only the two session tokens are used.
  • These tokens expire when you log out of ESPN or after a long period of inactivity. If your data stops updating, simply reconnect using fresh tokens from the steps above.
  • The connection is read-only — Roto Intel cannot make changes to your ESPN league.
Troubleshooting
Troubleshooting
  • Can't find the Application tab in DevTools? Make sure you're using Chrome or Edge. Safari and Firefox label this tab differently.
  • "Invalid credentials" error? The tokens may have expired — log out of ESPN, log back in, and copy fresh values.
  • Wrong league showing up? Double-check your League ID from the URL. If you're in multiple leagues, make sure you're copying the ID for the right one.
  • Ownership badges not appearing? Try disconnecting and reconnecting from Account settings.
03 — Yahoo

Connecting Your Yahoo League

Yahoo uses a standard one-click login. The whole process takes about 30 seconds.

Step-by-step
  1. 1

    In Roto Intel, click the person icon in the top-right corner to open Account settings.

  2. 2

    Under Fantasy Connections, click Connect Yahoo. You'll be taken to Yahoo's login page.

  3. 3

    Sign in with the Yahoo account you use for your fantasy league. If you're already logged in to Yahoo in this browser, you may skip straight past this step.

  4. 4

    Yahoo will show an authorisation screen asking if you want to give Roto Intel access to your fantasy data. Click Agree. This is read-only access — Roto Intel cannot make any changes to your league.

  5. 5

    You'll be redirected back to Roto Intel automatically. If you're in more than one Yahoo fantasy league, select the correct one from the dropdown that appears.

  6. 6

    Done. Your roster and league settings will sync within a few seconds. Ownership badges will appear across the platform and your Fantasy Dashboard will be ready to use.

Important notes
Notes
  • Your Yahoo password is never seen or stored by Roto Intel — authentication is handled entirely by Yahoo.
  • Yahoo access tokens expire periodically. If your data stops updating, just click Connect Yahoo again — it takes under 30 seconds to refresh.
  • Both public and private Yahoo leagues are supported.
  • Custom scoring settings for Yahoo leagues are coming soon as Yahoo support expands.
Troubleshooting
Troubleshooting
  • Redirected to Yahoo but nothing happens after logging in? Try clearing your browser cookies for yahoo.com and connecting again.
  • Wrong league showing? Disconnect from Account settings and reconnect — select the correct league from the dropdown on the way back in.
  • Data stopped updating? Your token has likely expired. Reconnect via Account settings to get a fresh one.
04 — Z-Scores

Understanding Z-Scores

The statistical foundation behind traditional rankings and projections.

What it's for

A z-score tells you how many standard deviations above or below average a player's stat is. One standard deviation covers 68% of the player pool — so a z-score between −1 and +1 is genuinely average. Two standard deviations covers 95%, meaning anything above +2 or below −2 puts a player in the top or bottom 2.5% of the league. Zero is exactly average.

How to read it
+2.0 and above
Elite — top 2.5% of the player pool
+1.0 to +2.0
Above average — meaningful positive contributor
−1.0 to +1.0
Average — 68% of players fall here
−1.0 to −2.0
Below average — drag on your roster
Below −2.0
Significant liability — bottom 2.5% of the player pool

The composite z-score sums all nine category z-scores into a single value representing overall fantasy value.

05 — Rankings

Rankings

A view of every player's fantasy value to date this season.

What it's for

The Rankings page ranks every NBA player by their composite z-score and CTW across all nine fantasy categories. Filter by position, time period (Full Season, Last 30, Last 14) and toggle between per game and totals views. Use it to find undervalued players, evaluate trade targets and identify streamers.

How to read it
  • The Value column is the composite z-score — your single best measure of overall fantasy worth.
  • The CTW (Contribution to Winning) column measures how much a player's stats contribute to winning head-to-head matchups, simulated across thousands of matchup scenarios. Where z-score measures statistical output, CTW measures winning impact.
  • Each category shows the raw average alongside its individual z-score in smaller text — green means above average, red means below.
  • The period selector changes which window the z-scores are calculated from — Last 14 Days surfaces recent form, Full Season smooths out variance.
  • The punt category toggle removes selected categories from the z-score calculation — use this if your team strategy involves punting FT% or turnovers.
06 — Projections

Projections

Forward-looking stat estimates for every player across any time horizon.

What it's for

The Projections page shows estimated stats for every player over a customisable future period. Unlike season averages which look backward, projections account for current role, minutes trends and upcoming schedule difficulty — applying per-opponent defensive factors across each player's scheduled games. Injury status is flagged alongside each player's projection. Use it to evaluate rest-of-season value and identify players whose role is changing.

How to read it
  • The projection figure is the central estimate — our best single guess at what a player will produce.
07 — Driver Analysis

Driver Analysis

Why a player's stats have shifted, and what's driving the change.

What it's for

Driver Analysis explains why a player's stats shift between two periods. It decomposes the change into its root causes: genuine skill rate change, role and usage change, and pace of play. It answers the question every serious fantasy player asks — is this real or noise?

How to read it
  • Baseline — the player's starting stat level in the comparison period.
  • Rate Change — change in the underlying skill rate. A negative rate change means genuine performance decline — this is the most important driver.
  • Role — change attributable to minutes or usage. A positive role contribution means more opportunity, not necessarily better performance.
  • Pace — external factor from team pace of play changes. Usually small.
  • Comparison — the final projected stat after all drivers are applied.
  • Green bars are positive contributors, red bars are negative.
08 — Trending

Trending Players

Who's rising and falling across every category right now.

What it's for

Trending player cards surface players whose stats are meaningfully moving relative to their season baseline. Each card shows the primary driver of the trend and the magnitude of change for the period.

How to read it
  • The headline figure shows the primary stat change driving the trend.
  • The driver tag (rate change, role, pace) tells you why the trend is happening — a role-driven trend is less reliable than a rate change-driven one.
  • Minutes and usage change are surfaced prominently — these are the leading indicators of fantasy value change.
09 — Matchup

Matchup Projection

Your weekly matchup, projected category by category with win probabilities.

What it's for

The Matchup Projection tool shows how your roster is projected to perform against your opponent this week across all nine categories. Rather than just projecting totals, it uses a statistical model to calculate a win probability for each category — projecting each team's expected totals as a distribution and calculating the probability your team comes out ahead, accounting for variance, games remaining, and player availability.

How to read it
  • Each category shows a win probability percentage. Above 60% is a likely win. Below 40% is a likely loss.
  • High-variance categories like STL and BLK will have wider probability bands than consistent categories like PTS and REB.
  • The what-if engine lets you simulate adding a free agent and dropping a player — all nine category win probabilities update to show the impact.
  • If you're mid-matchup, the tool accounts for stats already accumulated and projects only the remaining games.
10 — Trade Analysis

Trade Analysis

Simulate any trade or waiver move and see the impact on your roster.

What it's for

The Trade Analysis tool (currently ESPN only) simulates any roster change — trade or waiver pickup — and shows you the before and after across your entire squad. It calculates how your category totals shift, how your record changes against every opponent in the league, and where you'd sit in the projected standings.

How to read it
  • Click players on your roster to mark them as going out in a trade or being dropped. Search for incoming players by name.
  • The VS Each Opponent table shows your projected category win/loss record against every team, before and after the change.
  • The Squad Analysis section shows your full roster stats side by side — before and after — so you can see exactly which categories improve or decline.
  • Projected standings update to reflect where the change leaves you in the league table.
11 — Schedule

Schedule Grid

Every team's schedule at a glance, with ease ratings and fantasy week breakdown.

What it's for

The Schedule Grid shows every NBA team's upcoming games across the fantasy season. Colour coding indicates opponent ease based on points allowed. Use it to plan streaming decisions and identify which teams have the most games in a given week.

How to read it
  • Green cells indicate games against opponents who allow above-average stats — easier matchups for fantasy.
  • Red cells indicate games against strong defensive opponents.
  • The games played count per week is shown for each team — prioritise teams with 4+ game weeks.
  • The Weekly Schedule view breaks down each week day-by-day — teams as rows, Monday through Sunday as columns, with home/away indicator.
  • Fantasy playoff weeks are highlighted separately so you can plan ahead.
12 — CTW

Contribution to Winning

How much each player contributes to winning fantasy matchups, not just producing stats.

What it's for

CTW (Contribution to Winning) measures how much a player's stats contribute to winning head-to-head matchups. Where z-scores measure statistical output relative to the average player, CTW measures winning impact — specifically, how often a player's above-replacement contribution in a category is decisive in a real matchup. It's calculated by simulating the player's contribution across the league's actual team compositions.

How to read it
  • A positive CTW score means the player is a net contributor to winning categories across simulated matchups.
  • The by-category breakdown shows where a player's contribution is decisive versus negligible — useful for identifying punt-friendly players.
  • Players dominant in scarce categories (BLK, STL) can score higher CTW relative to their composite z-score, since those categories are harder to win league-wide.
  • CTW and z-score often agree, but diverge for players who concentrate their value in a small number of high-impact categories.
  • CTW appears as a column on both the Rankings and Projections pages.
FAQ

Frequently Asked Questions

How do I connect my ESPN or Yahoo league? ▾
See the Connecting Your ESPN League section for full step-by-step instructions. ESPN uses a cookie-based method that takes about two minutes on first setup. Yahoo uses a one-click OAuth login.
Does Roto Intel work for both ESPN and Yahoo leagues? ▾
ESPN is fully supported. Yahoo is supported for the Dashboard, Projected Standings, Roster Analysis and Trade Analysis. Matchup Projection for Yahoo is in development.
What scoring formats are supported? ▾
Roto Intel currently supports 9-category head-to-head and rotisserie formats on ESPN. Yahoo format support is in development.
How often is data updated? ▾
Player stats and injury news update in real time during games. Projections and rankings recalculate nightly. Box scores update automatically during live games via a persistent connection to the server.
What does a z-score actually mean? ▾
See the Understanding Z-Scores section above — we've written a full explainer there.
Is my league data private? ▾
Yes. Your league data is only used to personalise your Roto Intel experience and is never shared with other users or third parties.
What's included in the free tier? ▾
The free tier includes player rankings, projections, Driver Analysis, trending player cards, and box scores. League sync, matchup projection, trade analysis and the schedule grid require a Pro or Elite subscription.
Do I need to reconnect my league each season? ▾
For ESPN, your session cookies can expire if you log out of ESPN or after extended inactivity — just reconnect using fresh cookies from the ESPN connection guide. It takes about two minutes. Yahoo tokens expire periodically and refresh in under 30 seconds.
Can I connect a private league? ▾
Yes — private ESPN leagues are fully supported. Your session cookies give Roto Intel access automatically, with no extra steps required.
What happens if I change my ESPN or Yahoo password? ▾
For ESPN, changing your password will invalidate your existing session cookies. Just follow the ESPN connection steps again to grab fresh ones — it takes about two minutes. For Yahoo, your OAuth token will be invalidated — reconnect via Account settings in under 30 seconds.
